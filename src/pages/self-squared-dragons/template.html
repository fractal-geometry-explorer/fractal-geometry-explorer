Plates 185–192Self-Squared Dragons

Squaring a number is as elementary as arithmetic gets. Iterate it — with one constant added, z → λz(1−z) or x → x²−μ — and "Pandora's boxes of possibilities" open: dusts, dragons and whole atlases of shapes that the map leaves invariant, which Mandelbrot calls self-squared. This chapter is where the book draws the parameter maps now universally called the Mandelbrot set, published here and in Mandelbrot's 1980 paper for the first time. Every figure is computed from the rules in the captions.

The Cantor dust from a nonlinear map

f(x) = {½ − |x − ½|} / r, r = 1/3 · the largest bounded invariant set is the Cantor dust

Chapter 8 built the Cantor dust by cutting out middle thirds. Here the same dust appears with no cutting at all: it is the largest bounded set that the "inverted V" map leaves invariant. Start anywhere and iterate. Anything below 0 races to −∞; the open middle third rises above 1 and is thrown out on the next step, the middle thirds of what remains follow one step later — and what never leaves is exactly the dust. A nonlinear formula has swallowed the whole cascade, outer cutoff included.

Plates 188 and 189

The separators of z → λz(1−z) and z → z²−μ

black: the critical orbit stays bounded · μ = λ²/4 − λ/2 · the μ-map is the Mandelbrot set

The topology of a self-squared set depends on where the parameter lies with respect to a ramified curve S that Mandelbrot "discovered and now calls separator" — the connected boundary of the black shape. Inside S the open domain splits into atoms joined at bonds; atoms carry periods; bifurcation multiplies the period at every outbound bond crossing, and the "island molecules" offshore are shaped like the whole map. In the λ-map the corona of sprouts hangs on two empty discs, |λ| ≤ 1 and |λ−2| ≤ 1; the receptor bonds sit at λ = exp(2πim/n) and λ = 2 − exp(2πim/n).

Plates 190 and 191

Self-squared dragons; approach to the "Peano" limit

λ in a sprout at θ = 2πm/n → n heads around every articulation · heft and twist set by the cycle's multiplier

"To watch a dragon in the process of self-squaring would be a fascinating sight!" — a molting that detaches the skin of belly and back, stretches it to twice its length, folds it around both sides and re-attaches every fold. The best dragons obtain where λ lies in a sprout of Plate 189 at θ/2π = m/n: around each articulation point there are n heads or tails. A nice heft comes from sitting well inside the sprout, a nice twist from leaning toward a subsprout; "starved" of flesh by infinitely many bifurcations, a dragon collapses into a skeletal branched curve, and in an offshore island it becomes a σ-dragon. As n → ∞ the sprouts crowd toward λ = 1 and the dragons strain toward a "hermit-dragon" filling a scallop shell to the brim — a self-squared Peano curve, D = 2, which dies as a curve the moment it gets there.

Plate 192

Real self-squared Fatou dusts on [0, 1]

λ > 4 · −4/λ runs from −1 up to 0 · black: the first k iterates stay in [0, 1]

Fatou 1906 — "a masterpiece of an odd literary genre," the squirreled-away Comptes Rendus note — points out that when λ is real and λ > 4 (or λ < −2), the largest bounded invariant set of λx(1−x) is a dust in [0, 1]: a close relative of the Cantor dust, with the map itself supplying the cascade. Each horizontal line of this diagram is one λ; the mid trema is where the first step throws x above 1, its end points x₁ and x₂ solve λx(1−x) = 1 and draw a parabola; the preimages of the tremas open the tremas of every following order.
