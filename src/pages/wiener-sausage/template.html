The trail

Chapter 25 builds the planar Brown trail as a random Peano curve. Start with a random segment whose mean-square length is 1. At each pass, push the midpoint of every side in a random direction by a Gaussian distance whose mean square halves from pass to pass. Two halves, each 1/√2 the size of the whole in mean square, give the dimension log 2 / log √2 = 2.

A stage here is two passes: stage n has 4n steps, each about 2−n long. Lengths are in units where the whole path's mean-square span is 1.

Wiener's sausage

Plate 32's caption passes on a rumor that the word "sausage" comes from drawing this band around Norbert Wiener's Brownian curves. Probability theory kept the name. In the plane the band's expected area does not fall like a power of ε. It falls only like 1 / log(1/ε): in these units, about π / ln(1/2ε²), a result of Spitzer (1964).

Why the fitted D falls short of 2

A dimension of 2 would make area ÷ 2ε grow like 1/ε, a straight line of slope −1. The logarithm bends it: the local slope is about −1 + 2 / ln(1/2ε²), so a fit over practical values of ε gives roughly 1.7 to 1.8. D = 2 is reached only in the limit, and only logarithmically. It is the same reason page 235 gives for a strange fact: the trail has dimension 2, yet its area is zero.

The critical stage

Plate 32's point holds here too. The band keeps changing while a stage adds steps longer than ε, and stops once the new steps are shorter. Turn on the highlight and step through the stages: the moved area shrinks to slivers once 2−n falls below ε.
