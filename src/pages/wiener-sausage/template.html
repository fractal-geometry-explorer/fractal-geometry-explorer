A Brown trail in its Wiener sausage

D = log 2 / log √2 = 2 · expected band area ≈ π / ln(1/2ε²), Spitzer 1964

Chapter 25 builds the planar Brown trail as a random Peano curve. Start with a random segment whose mean-square length is 1. At each pass, push the midpoint of every side in a random direction by a Gaussian distance whose mean square halves from pass to pass. Two halves, each 1/√2 the size of the whole in mean square, give the dimension log 2 / log √2 = 2. A stage here is two passes: stage n has 4n steps, each about 2−n long, in units where the whole path's mean-square span is 1.

Reading D from the band

log–log

Plate 32's caption passes on a rumor that the word "sausage" comes from drawing this band around Norbert Wiener's Brownian curves; probability theory kept the name. In the plane the band's expected area does not fall like a power of ε. It falls only like 1 / log(1/ε) — in these units, about π / ln(1/2ε²), a result of Spitzer (1964). Each line is one stage: band area ÷ 2ε, the path's length as seen at resolution ε. The lines share one path at large ε and level off once ε drops below that stage's steps. The dashed line has the slope a dimension of 2 would give; the dotted curve is Wiener's law for the expected area. The measured lines follow the bend of the dotted curve, which is why the fitted D (current stage, ε up to 0.03) stays below 2.

A dimension of 2 would make area ÷ 2ε grow like 1/ε, a straight line of slope −1. The logarithm bends it: the local slope is about −1 + 2 / ln(1/2ε²), so a fit over practical values of ε gives roughly 1.7 to 1.8. D = 2 is reached only in the limit, and only logarithmically. It is the same reason page 235 gives for a strange fact: the trail has dimension 2, yet its area is zero.
