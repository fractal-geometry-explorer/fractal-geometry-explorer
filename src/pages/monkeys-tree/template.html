The generator

Start with a straight segment and replace it with an 11-step zigzag: six steps a third as long as the segment and five steps 1/(3√3) as long, all pointing along multiples of 30°. Then replace each of those steps with a smaller copy of the whole zigzag, and keep going.

Each copy can go down four ways: as drawn, mirrored, reversed, or reversed and mirrored. Mandelbrot's arrangement is one of 411, about 4.2 million, and it keeps the curve from ever crossing itself. Most others tangle within two levels.

Why D = 1.8687

With pieces of two sizes, the dimension is the exponent that makes the scaled pieces add up to exactly one whole:

6·(1/3)D + 5·(1/(3√3))D = 1
D = 1.86873

Every level multiplies the curve's length by 2 + 5/(3√3) ≈ 2.962, so the length grows without limit while the outline barely moves. It is the coastline paradox from the same chapter.

The fill

The plate shades everything on the left of the curve as you walk it from start to finish, then closes the shape with a solid band underneath. Where the curve dips below its starting line, white hooks bite into the band.

The book's drawing is level 3, with 1,331 segments, and its corners are drawn as smooth curls. Set Arc rounding and White channels to zero to see the exact polygon underneath.

Minkowski sausage

Plate 32, on the next page of the book, wraps a curve in a band of radius ε: every point within ε of it. The band's area divided by its width 2ε is the curve's length as seen at resolution ε. For a smooth curve that settles to the true length as ε shrinks. For a fractal it keeps growing, roughly like ε1−D, which is the law Richardson found for coastlines. Choose Sausage and drag ε to measure it here.

The curls, as circular arcs

Each segment of the polygon is redrawn as two circular arcs that pass through its endpoints and meet with matching tangents, a construction called a biarc. At every vertex the two neighbouring biarcs share one tangent, so the outline is a single chain of circles with no kinks.

The tangent at a vertex splits the turn there, leaning slightly toward the longer neighbouring segment. That rule, a band 0.295 of the base deep, and a hairline white channel gave the closest overlap with a scan of the plate. Lower Arc rounding to turn the arcs back toward straight lines.
