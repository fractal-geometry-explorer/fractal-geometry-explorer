Plate 42

Original construction by Helge von Koch

Coastline dimension D = log 4 / log 3 ≈ 1.26186

Start with a black equilateral triangle. On the middle third of every side, paste a triangular peninsula a third as wide. The triangle becomes a Star of David, then a 48-sided island, and so on forever. The first triangle's corners never move. Every other point either settles after finitely many stages or keeps moving by ever smaller amounts toward the limit coastline.

Each stage multiplies the coastline by 4/3, so its length grows without limit, while the area converges to 8/5 of the first triangle.

Why a tangent cannot be defined here

Fix a corner of the first triangle and draw the chord to a point on the coastline. Move the point toward the corner. Near the corner the coastline is a copy of itself three times smaller, so the chord swings back and forth inside a 30° wedge forever and never settles on a direction. Mandelbrot calls such a point hyperbolic.

Chord angle below the side0.0°

The angle repeats every time the point gets 3 times closer, between 0° and 30°, without ever converging.

Plate 43

Alternative construction by Ernest Cesàro

Coastline dimension D = log 4 / log 3 ≈ 1.26186

Cesàro squeezes the coastline from both sides. A white hexagon of swamp surrounds a black Star of David of land, in a gray sea. At each stage a tent-shaped notch is pushed into every side, alternately from the outside and the inside, so chunks of swamp go to the sea or to the land. In the limit the swamp shrinks from an area down to a curve: the Koch coastline.

Each stage hands 5/9 of the remaining swamp to land or sea: the swamp is (2/3)(4/9)k of the first triangle, so it vanishes while the coastline's length grows without limit. The tent applied twice, once outward and once inward, is exactly Koch's four-piece generator.

Plate 44

Two kinds of self-similarity: standard and fractal

D = log N / log (1/r)

Cut a segment into b equal pieces, or a square into b² equal squares, and the formula log N / log(1/r) gives 1 and 2: the ordinary dimensions. A third of the Koch coastline also splits into exact copies of itself, 4 of them, each a third as large. The same formula then gives log 4 / log 3, a number no shape of standard geometry has.

Cut the Koch curveinto 4 pieces

Hausdorff showed this D is also the curve's Hausdorff dimension. Mandelbrot's claim is that it matters in natural science too.

Triadic Koch lake

Coastline D = log 4 / log 3 ≈ 1.26186 · the waves: a plane-filling sweep, D = 2

Plate 42's island printed as a photographic negative: a lake in a black disc. The gray waves come from a curve Mandelbrot designed later (Plates 68–69), a Peano curve that sweeps out the whole snowflake. Each step is rounded into a sixth of a circle, and one side of the curve is shaded.
