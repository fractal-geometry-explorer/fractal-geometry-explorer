Plates II, VIII, X, 467Update for the Second Printing

Between delivery and the second printing, "fractal geometry did not stand still: it moved on at increasing speed." The update Mandelbrot bound into the 1982 reprint reports a year of news — metal fractures measured by fractal dimension, a new map z → λ(z−1/z) iterated beside the old dragons, and the squaring map carried into Hamilton's quaternions. Its four plates hide in the front matter and after the index; every figure here is computed from the text's rules.

Plate VIII, facing the foreword

A Julia set of z → λ(z−1/z)

basins of an attracting cycle, shaded by phase · the Julia set is the boundary between them

The update's chaos section iterates a map built from a pole instead of a critical polynomial: z → λ(z−1/z). "An example of Julia set defined as the boundary of the basins of attraction" is its Plate VIII. When the critical orbit settles on an attracting cycle, every point of the plane falls toward one of the cycle's points; shading each basin by the phase at which it arrives tiles the plane with rounded domains, and the Julia set — chains of starred clusters around the pole's preimages — is the fence between them.

Plate X, before the foreword

The λ-map of z → λ(z−1/z)

unit circle: beyond it ∞ attracts · white discs |λ∓½| ≤ ½: one and two limit points · between: atoms and dust

"To study other λ's, a map analogous to Plates 188 and 189 was drawn, and part of it is shown on Plate X. We observe a very interesting form of universality: the island molecules in Plate X take precisely the same form as for the quadratic mapping — Plates X and 188–189 are built using the same building blocks." The whole map lives inside the unit circle (outside it the iteration "converges to infinity except for points z₀ forming a dust"). Two big discs hang inside it, one with a single limit point and one — the caption's white disc — with two; between the discs and the circle runs the black corona: sprouted atoms of every larger cycle, strung on a dust of chaotic λ.

Plate II (page ii) and Plate 467

The squaring map in quaternions

q → q² − μ, μ complex · the complex-plane section is a dragon of Chapter 19 · drag to turn

A. Norton suggested that after complex z "a next most natural environment is Hamilton's quaternions", and drew the first pictures (Norton 1982). The set of quaternions that fail to iterate to infinity lives in four dimensions; what prints is a three-dimensional section, shaded. "Each major segment of the dragon obtained by complex plane section is imbedded in a major segment of the spatial shape… surrounded by multiple loosely fitting belts" — Plate 467, a cycle of size 4. Plate II, facing the half-title, "shows a different spatial fractal obtained in roughly the same fashion." Even noncommutativity turned into an asset: each variant way of writing the map induces different links between the domains, so the topology needs extra information to pin down.

Page 461

Metal fractures and fractals

Mandelbrot, Passoja & Paullay · spectra and slit islands give nearly the same D · impact energy falls as D grows

The update's one data figure brings fractals to the materials lab. A fracture surface of steel is "plated with electroless nickel" and polished down, so islands of metal appear in a sea of nickel — artificial "slit islands" whose area–perimeter plot (Chapter 12's trick) is "very nearly straight". A spectrum taken along the surface gives a second, independent D, and |D(spectral) − D(islands)| comes out "of the order of a few hundredths". Heat-treat the same Maraging steel at different temperatures and the Charpy impact energy falls as D rises: the first engineering use of fractal dimension as a material parameter.

Pages 458–466

The rest of the update, mapped to its chapters

every other topic reports news on a chapter this site already rebuilds

  • The definition of "fractal". Mandelbrot regrets the 1977 pedantic definition and returns to the 1975 stance: leave "fractal" without one, use "fractal dimension" generically for all the variants of Chapter 39, and pick whichever fits each case.
  • Homogeneous fractal turbulence. Numerical support for D ≈ 2.5–2.6 continues (Chorin), and Hentschel & Procaccia connect the stretching vortices of Chapter 10 to the polymers of Chapter 36.
  • Cloud and rain areas. Lovejoy & Mandelbrot build fractal rain maps on the finding that rainfall discontinuities follow the same hyperbolic law as the price jumps of Chapter 37; the area–perimeter data is Plate 115.
  • Earthquakes. Kagan & Knopoff's statistics make quake patterns scaling and fractal, as the relief model of Chapter 28 prepares one to expect; Omori saw scaling in aftershocks nearly a century earlier.
  • Fractal electrodes. Le Méhauté: a battery stores by volume and discharges by surface, so electrode design "poses a fractal problem" — the length–area–volume tension of Chapter 12 turned into engineering.
  • Percolation confirmed. Kapitulnik, Aharony, Deutscher & Stauffer recover D ≈ 1.9 for the contact clusters predicted in Chapter 13, and thin gold and lead films percolate "on the dot".
  • Low-lacunarity lattices. Gefen, Meir, Mandelbrot & Aharony build the physicists' postulated fractional-dimension spaces as Sierpiński carpets whose lacunarity (Chapter 34) tends to zero. The gasket, meanwhile, becomes the "physicists' toy" of exact renormalization — and Mandelbrot grumbles that a French dictionary turned his gasket into ship's rope; he prefers tamis, sieve.
  • Cellular automata. The neighbor-rule example on p. 328 (Chapter 36) is a cellular automaton, and Willson, Wolfram and Vichniac observe that automaton output "can, in fact, be fractal".
  • z → z²−μ, proved. Douady & Hubbard prove the μ-map of Chapter 19 — the set now carrying Mandelbrot's name — is connected, by mapping its exterior onto that of a circle; Ruelle proves the dimension of a Julia dragon analytic in the parameter.

The update opens with the Courchevel workshop of July 1982 and closes with eleven columns of fresh references. Only the topics above left no figure of their own; the tracker's review pass can decide whether any deserves more than this map.

The front matter of the updated printing holds no other plates: page i is the half-title, and the plates face pages ii, viii and x — there is no "Plate I". The red endpaper fractal inside the covers is printed without caption or credit.
