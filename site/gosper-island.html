Plates 46–47Gosper Island

W. Gosper's variant of the Koch island. It starts from a hexagon, and each side is replaced by three sides 1/√7 as long, again and again. The area never changes, copies of the island cover the plane, every island splits into seven smaller copies of itself, and a single Peano curve fills it. Each figure is computed from these rules so you can take it apart.

The generator is symmetric about its center point, so each peninsula it pushes out is matched by a bay of exactly the same area. The coastline grows by 3/√7 ≈ 1.134 at every stage, so the limit coastline is infinitely long. The area stays 3√3/2 for a hexagon of side 1.

Tiling and pertiling

Copies of the Gosper island cover the plane with no gaps and no overlaps, which is called tiling. Better still, each island divides into seven provinces, and every province is the whole island shrunk by 1/√7 and turned 19.1°. Mandelbrot coined pertiling for this. Seven hexagons never quite make a bigger hexagon; Gosper's island bends the hexagon just enough that seven islands make a bigger island.

Why a tangent cannot be defined at any point

Fix a corner of the first hexagon and draw the chord from it to a point on the coastline. Near the corner the coastline is a copy of itself √7 times smaller and turned 19.1°. So each time the point gets √7 times closer, the chord turns another 19.1°. Coming from either side it winds around the corner without end, always clockwise. Mandelbrot calls such a point loxodromic; every point the coastline reaches at a finite stage is one.

Plate 47

Gosper island with variable-width hatching

Coastline D ≈ 1.1291 · the filling: a Peano curve, D = 2

An advanced stage of the island, hatched by the filling curve at stage 5 in lines of three widths. Thicker lines read as darker gray, so the seven provinces show up as tones: a light center ringed by six copies, alternately dark and medium. The whole hatching is one unbroken line. Mandelbrot adds that the outline of France, often called the Hexagon, looks more like this plate than like a hexagon.
